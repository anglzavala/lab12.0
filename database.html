1. Introduction to Databases

What is a database?

An organized collection of information that can be stored, searched, updated, and managed efficiently.

Why are databases important?

They help people and organizations manage large amounts of data consistently, securely, and reliably.

What is a DBMS?

A Database Management System is software for creating, querying, securing, and maintaining databases.

Database vs. spreadsheet

Databases support structured relationships, concurrent users, and integrity rules. Spreadsheets are convenient for small grids, calculations, and individual analysis.

Databases in everyday life

Universities manage student records; banks process accounts and transactions; online stores track products and orders; hospitals maintain patient records; social platforms store profiles and posts; libraries track books and loans.

5. Database Types and Applications
Relational
Stores structured data in related tables and uses SQL. Strong constraints and joins; common for finance and business systems. Examples: MySQL, PostgreSQL, SQL Server.
NoSQL
Uses flexible models such as documents or key-value pairs. Useful for variable data and some high-scale workloads. Examples: MongoDB and Redis.
Cloud
Hosted/managed database services provide network access, backups, and flexible capacity. Examples: Amazon RDS and Google Cloud SQL.
Distributed
Stores and processes data across multiple networked machines for scale and resilience. Example: Apache Cassandra.
