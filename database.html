1. Introduction to Databases

What is a database?

An organized collection of structured data designed for efficient storage, retrieval, and management.

Why are databases important?

They support large-scale data, accurate updates, access control, relationships, and reliable transactions.

What is a DBMS?

A Database Management System is software used to define, manage, secure, and interact with databases.

Databases vs. spreadsheets

Databases manage complex relationships, concurrent users, and integrity rules. Spreadsheets are primarily grid-based analysis tools.

Where are databases used?

University records, banking, online stores, hospitals, social media, libraries, government systems, and transportation networks.

3. Database Structure and Relationships

Core terms

Table: organized collection of records.
Row: one record.
Column: one field.
Primary key: uniquely identifies a row.
Foreign key: references a primary key in another table.

Relationship

A major can have many students, while each student has one major. This is a one-to-many relationship.

5. Database Types and Applications
Relational
MySQL: structured tables and SQL. Strong integrity and relationships. Used in commerce and business applications.
NoSQL
MongoDB / Redis: flexible documents or fast key-value storage. Used for catalogs, caching, sessions, and real-time apps.
Cloud databases
Managed database services run by providers, offering scaling, backups, and availability. Example: Amazon RDS or Google Cloud SQL.
Distributed databases
Data is stored across networked locations for scale and resilience. Example: Apache Cassandra.
