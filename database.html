Database fundamentals

Start with the building blocks and see why databases power everyday services.

What is a database?

An organized collection of information that can be stored, searched, updated, and managed efficiently.

Why do we use databases?

They keep information consistent and make it practical to search, share, secure, and update data at scale.

What is a DBMS?

A Database Management System is software for creating, querying, securing, and maintaining a database.

Database or spreadsheet?

Spreadsheets are great for small grids and analysis. Databases are designed for structured relationships, reliable updates, permissions, and many users.

Databases in everyday life

Universities track students and courses · Banks process accounts and transactions · Online stores manage products and orders · Hospitals maintain patient information · Social platforms store profiles and posts · Libraries track books and loans.

Tables and relationships

Related tables reduce repeated information and connect records through keys.

How a table is organized

A table groups related data. Each row is a record; each column is a field. A primary key uniquely identifies a row. A foreign key references a key in another table.

One major, many students

Each student refers to a major. One major may be associated with many students, creating a one-to-many relationship.

Database types
Different systems make different trade-offs to suit their use cases.
Relational
Structured tables, relationships, and SQL. Strong constraints and consistency. Used in finance and business systems. Examples: MySQL, PostgreSQL, SQL Server.
NoSQL
Flexible data models such as documents or key-value pairs. Useful for varied data structures and specific scaling needs. Examples: MongoDB, Redis.
Cloud databases
Hosted or managed services provide network access, backups, and flexible capacity. Examples: Amazon RDS, Google Cloud SQL.
Distributed databases
Data is stored across multiple networked machines to support scale and resilience. Example: Apache Cassandra.
